My namemeans clear.

I'm Jasna Dimitrovska. I'm a psychologist and a Gestalt counsellor, I have sung for as long as I can remember, and I live in Bitola, in the south of Macedonia, at the foot of Mount Pelister.

The story

I.

Singing came first.

Before I could read, I sang: in my grandmother's kitchen, in the echo of our stairwell, on the bus with my forehead against the glass. Singing wasn't a hobby. It was the way I thought.

Then, somewhere around thirteen, I started singing more quietly, and then not in front of anyone at all. It took me years to understand why. I wrote about it in the first entry of the journal.

These days I sing on stage, as a vocalist with Orchestra DIVA in Bitola, in concerts from MTV hits of the 80s and 90s to Stars Symphony and Vienna in Bitola. The voice I used to hide now has a microphone and an orchestra behind it.

II.

Then came the words.

I studied psychology because I wanted names for what I felt. Attachment. Shame. The spotlight effect. Attunement. The names helped: something with a name can be looked at.

But I kept noticing that a song often reached a feeling faster than a definition did, and that the two together reached it best.

Today I'm a psychologist and a Gestalt counsellor. Gestalt work begins with awareness: noticing what is happening here and now, in the body as much as in words, and in the contact between people. In its own way, it is a practice of listening.

I'm also drawn to the quieter costs of doing well. In 2024 I co-authored a published study, Self-esteem as a mediator between perfectionism and impostor syndrome among gifted students (International Slavic University), on perfectionism, self-esteem and impostor feelings.

Jasnota isn't where I counsel anyone, though. Here, what I can do is explain, and listen, and sing.

III.

A name that splits in two.

jasI, the self
+
notaa note

In Macedonian, jasna means clear, bright, lucid. It is also my name. Jasnota sounds like jasnost, clarity, and it splits into jas and nota: I, and a note. The self and the sound in one word.

I didn't plan that. I found it, the way you find a harmony that was already in the song.

IV.

Bitola, in 7/8.

I live and write in Bitola. The music I grew up with doesn't count in fours. It counts 2 + 2 + 3, and people still dance to it without thinking. I think about that a lot: how something uneven can still keep time.

Jasnota is written from here, in English and in Macedonian, for anyone who listens. You don't need to know where Bitola is. You only need to have had a feeling you couldn't name.

What this is,and what it isn't.

  • Essays about the inner life, written plainly.
  • Conversations about the music that shaped people.
  • Recordings: songs, breathing, my voice and the silence around it.
  • One letter a month.
  • Not therapy. I'm a psychologist and a Gestalt counsellor, but I'm not your therapist, and there are no sessions here.
  • Not a diagnosis, or advice for your situation.
  • Not motivation. Nothing here asks you to become a better version of anything.
  • Not in a hurry.

If you are struggling, please talk to a psychologist, a psychotherapist or a doctor. Jasnota can keep you company. It can't replace care.
